# How California Bird Atlas is built

A reference for whoever works on the site next. Day-to-day use is in `README.md`.

## The short version

- **Hosting:** one Cloudflare Worker (`src/worker.js`) plus static files (`public/`). Pushing to `main` on GitHub deploys it.
- **Database:** Cloudflare D1 (SQLite). Photos are stored in D1 too until R2 is switched on in `wrangler.jsonc`.
- **Front end:** plain JavaScript modules, no framework and no build step. What is in `public/` is what the browser gets.
- **Checks:** `npm run check` validates the data files, stress-tests the bird draw and syntax-checks every script.

## The two kinds of bird data

The site keeps bird information in two places, joined by **common name**:

1. **The field guide** (`public/data/wildindex.json`): fixed reference data. Plate number, family, category, and a level for each of 7 regions in each of 4 seasons. It changes only when the levels are revised, by rerunning `tools/wildindex/export_site_data.py`.
2. **The database** (`species` table): things that change while the site runs. Reference photo links, the editor's notes, and which photos and sightings belong to each bird.

In the browser these are `wi()` (field guide) and `S.species` (database). `S.spByName` and `dbSpecies(bird)` join them. `npm run check` fails if the two lists of names ever differ.

## Levels, regions and seasons

- Regions, always in this order: `BAY, NC, CV, SN, CC, SC, DES`.
- Seasons: `W` winter (from 1 Dec), `P` spring (1 Mar), `S` summer (1 Jun), `F` fall (1 Sep), on the California calendar.
- A bird's levels are four 7-character strings, one per season, one character per region: `1` easy, `2` takes a trip, `3` hard, `-` not dependable. Read them with `levelOf(bird, regionIndex, season)`.
- Categories (`cat`): `assign` can be drawn for an assignment; `scarce`, `rare`, `night`, `offshore` score but are never assigned; `sensitive` and `introduced` never score.

## The nine birds

Nothing about an assignment is stored. `rotation.js` recomputes it from the season, year and region, so every visitor gets the same nine and there is nothing to keep in sync. A season has six assignments (five of two weeks, then one to the season's end). Each level's eligible birds are shuffled once per season with a seeded generator and dealt three at a time.

## The Bird Wall

Every bird on the wall is a row in `submissions`, whatever its source:

| Source | How it arrives | `status` | `sample` |
|---|---|---|---|
| A reader | Send a photo (`POST /api/submissions`) | `pending`, until published in Review | 0 |
| The editor | Add birds (`POST /api/wall`) | `approved` at once | 0 |
| Sample fill | Review, "Fill with sample birds" | `approved` | 1 |

A bird shows on the wall when it is `approved` and has a cut-out. Each has three image files: `thumb`, `display` (2,560 px at most) and `cutout`. While the table has no approved birds, the wall shows the static samples in `wall-samples.json` instead.

## Issues, stories and awards

- An **issue** (`issues` table) is a season's edition: number, title, theme, a call for photos, and a submission window. `public/js/issues.js` works out whether it is open, upcoming or closed.
- Every new photo is tagged with the issue whose window is open, or the next one to open (`issueFor` in the worker).
- The submission form asks four **Behind the Shot** questions. The answers are kept in `submissions.story` and joined into a first-draft `caption`, which the editor can rewrite in Review. Readers only ever see `caption`.
- An **award** is a line of text on a submission (for example "Best behavior"), set in Review. Award winners lead the issue's page.
- `/issues` shows the current issue; `/issues/<number>` shows any issue.

## The two-week email

- Readers join with an email address and a region (`subscribers` table; only the editor can read it). `/unsubscribe` deletes an address.
- `public/js/email.js` writes the email for a region from the current assignment. Review shows it with the region's addresses, ready to copy into an email app and send by BCC.
- **Nothing is sent automatically.** That needs a mail service (such as Resend), a domain the site owns, and a scheduled trigger on the Worker. The generator is already separate from the page, so adding sending later is a small step.

## Scoreboard and missing birds

`/board` counts everything from the public wall list: species per region, and which scoring birds have no photo yet. Sample birds and non-scoring birds are ignored. No accounts are involved.

## Words the site uses

One name per thing, everywhere: an **assignment** is the two-week set of **nine birds**; an **issue** is a season's edition; the **Bird Wall** is the public gallery; **My Wall** is the private one. Avoid "card" for assignments.

## Privacy rules built into the code

- The region is chosen by the reader and kept in their browser. Nothing about location is collected.
- Photos are re-encoded in the browser before upload, which removes embedded location data.
- Visitors never receive coordinates, place names or notes (`publicPhoto`, `publicObs` in the worker).
- Only the editor's signed session cookie allows changes. There is no other way in.

## Known limits

- **Styles are layered.** `magazine.css` was restyled several times, and later rules override earlier ones. Unused rules have been removed, but the overrides have not been folded together. It works and is documented; a full rewrite would be a tidy-up, not a fix.
- **Photo storage.** D1 is fine for hundreds of wall birds. Switch on R2 (see `wrangler.jsonc`) before thousands.
- **No accounts.** Points, ratings and leaderboards are described on the site but not recorded yet.
- **Spam.** The submission form is limited to 12 photos a day per visitor and nothing more.
- **The old Gallery page** (`/gallery`) still works but is not in the menu.

## Languages

The public pages can be read in English, Spanish, Chinese (Traditional), Tagalog, Vietnamese and Korean, the
languages most spoken in California. Views are written in English only. After a page is built, `public/js/i18n.js`
walks its text and swaps any line it has a translation for (and watches for text added later, like pop-ups).
- Translations live in `tools/i18n/strings.txt`, one line per piece of text. Rebuild with
  `python3 tools/i18n/build.py`, which writes `public/data/i18n.json`.
- Text with numbers, dates or region names in it is handled by `PATTERNS` in i18n.js.
- Left in English on purpose: bird and family names, photographers' captions and stories, issue titles and
  blurbs, the About page, the scoring table, and the editor's tool pages. Put `data-noi18n` on anything that must
  never be translated.
- The translations were written without a native-speaker review. Have each one checked before a big launch.

## Reader features (round 3)

- **Hearts** (`public/js/hearts.js`, worker route `hearts`, table `hearts`): one heart per photo per browser,
  toggled with POST /api/hearts/<id>. No accounts; the browser keeps a random id. At most 300 hearts a day from one
  connection. `/api/wall` returns each photo's count.
- **Readers' Choice**: on each issue page, the photo with the most hearts (at least `MIN_HEARTS`, 3). Final when
  the issue closes.
- **Photo of the week** (`photoOfTheWeek` in views/cover.js): under the cover bird. Changes every Monday,
  California time, by itself. Picks from recent photos with a story, most-hearted first.
- **Stamps** (`public/js/stamps.js`): on My Wall. Worked out from My Wall birds and photos sent from this browser,
  both of which now record the region. Nothing new is stored.
- **Light today** (`public/js/sun.js`): sunrise, sunset and golden hour on This Season, calculated in the browser
  from NOAA's formulas for one town per region. Daylight saving is handled by formatting in America/Los_Angeles.
  Nothing to maintain.
- **Full screen and swipe** (`public/js/viewer.js`): full-screen photos without stretching; swipe between birds
  on bird pages and in the wall's photo panel (arrow keys too).
- **Bird pages**: "Play the year" steps the map through the seasons; "Compare side by side" for look-alikes.
- **Photographer card**: inside the wall's photo panel (photos, species, hearts, regions, more of their birds).

## Accessibility

Built to Apple's Human Interface Guidelines and Google's Material/Android guidance, checked with axe-core
(WCAG 2.2 AA):
- every tap target is at least 44 × 44 px, 48 px on touch screens;
- a two-tone focus ring that shows on black and on paper; a "Skip to the page" link;
- dialogs keep focus inside and hand it back when they close;
- text contrast at least 4.5 : 1; colour never carries meaning alone (levels also have numbers or words);
- motion stops when the reader's device asks for reduced motion; the cover never auto-plays then;
- zoom is never blocked; `<html lang>` follows the chosen language.
To re-check after changes, run axe on each public page at phone and desktop widths.

## The Count (/progress)

The editor's progress toward photographing every bird in California (`public/js/views/progress.js`). A bird counts
once there is a confirmed photo of it in the atlas tools, or the editor has put it on the Bird Wall from /add
(`submissions.by_editor = 1`). The goal, meaning which kinds of birds count, is the editor's Settings choice
(native residents and migrants by default). Rare visitors photographed outside the goal are shown as a bonus line.
Bird pages say whether each bird is on the count. Nothing is entered by hand.
